## COVID-19 Case Rates by Zipcode, New York City, 2020
! This map shows the rate of cases at which they were reported for different zipcodes in New York City(images/lab2_layout_edwards.png)
**Question:** Where in New York City were COVID-19 case rates highest and lowest in 2020
**Data:** The dataset name is COVID-19 ZCTA, was published by NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene in 2020, “COVID19_ZCTA.” ArcGIS Online, Esri, services3.arcgis.com/HHsRcAgIU6SqBiQd/arcgis/rest/services/COVID19_ZCTA/FeatureServer. Accessed 11 Oct. 2026
**Method:** I used ArcGIS Pro to add the COVID19_ZCTA hosted feature layer from ArcGIS Online to a new map project. I symbolized the COVID Case Rate field with Graduated Colors, using Natural Breaks with five classes and a sequential color ramp. Each ZIP code’s rate is shown instead of its raw case count. I then built a layout with a title, legend, north arrow, scale bar, and source note.
**A design choice I made and why:** I decided to use 5 classes instead of 3, 7, or more. I did this because If I only had 3 classes it would inacurratly show how the rates were distributed and less than 7 so it's a lot easier to read
**A limitation of this map:** This map shows the rate accumulated through the year. It does not show if one zipcode got most their cases within a timeline, just what the rate was for the year overall. Some zipcodes could've gotten most the cases in a month and tappered off but the map doesnt show that

## The 2018 Kilauea eruption StoryMap
! This map shows the different phases of the eruption and how it affected the area around it. Also shows how I made the map visable to the human eye(images/video_project_2.mp4)
*Interactive version, live as of September 2026: (https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/9cbce01880b6409485f781d11fa9700c)*
**Question:** How did the 2018 Kīlauea volcanic eruption physically change the coastline and land area of the affected region over time
**Data:** The dataset name is Kilauea 2018 Lava Flow Extents, was published by U.S. Geological Survey in 2019, Kilauea 2018 Lava Flow Extents courtesy of ESRI: Learn ArcGIS
**Method:** I used ArcGIS Online Map Viewer to add four Landsat 8 images of the 2018 Kīlauea eruption, plus the Kilauea 2018 Lava Flow Extents layer, then styled them as natural color 4, 3, 2 and shortwave infrared 7, 5, 4 and adjusted the brightness and contrast called SWIR. The combination showed more of the active lava through the smoke in the July and August images. I then built an ArcGIS StoryMap with a sidecar for the four dates and a swipe map comparing March 2018 to February 2019, and published it publicly
**A design choice I made and why:** I explained where the lava was visible because to answer the geographic question, you have to understand how the lava was working during those different phases. I left out where it didnt go to highlight the geogeaphic question at hand
**A limitation of this map:** This map does not show a live view of what the eruption looked like for the people down on the ground. It did not show the specific effect it had on the surrounding people.
